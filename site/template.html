Can a robot tell from force alone that a lens is seated, after its fixture has moved?

Jiya Udaybhai Parmar (26250019), ES 678 Machine Learning, IIT Gandhinagar, November 2026

Within one recording session, yes: nearest neighbours on the force curves separate seated from unseated lenses with ROC-AUC 0.855. Trained on one session and tested on the other, the same model falls to chance (0.479), because the fixture moved and turned between sessions. Describing the hold phase with quantities that a moved fixture provably cannot change brings cross-session AUC back to 0.698 to 0.760 with the same learners, at a measured cost within a session.

1The question

A Franka FR3 arm pushes a transparent spectacle lens into the groove of a frame (the wedge), holds still for about 5 s (the hold) and then does a harder snap-fit. Snap-fitting a lens that is not seated can crack it, and a camera cannot see whether a transparent lens is seated. The decision has to come from force.

  • One row is one trial: one wedge attempt and the hold that follows it.
  • Target: 1 if the operator labelled the trial a success (seated), 0 for failure or partial contact, because neither is safe to snap-fit.
  • When the prediction is made: after the hold, before the snap-fit, from the force and motion logged up to that moment.

The question has two parts. First, does the force signal discriminate seated from unseated lenses? Second, does that still hold in a new session where the fixture sits somewhere else? Deployment needs the second, and ordinary random cross-validation cannot answer it, because every training fold then contains trials from both sessions.

Limits of the claim. One robot, one frame and lens type, two sessions, one labelling operator. The results are predictive, not causal.

2The data

Source and access. Force recordings from my internship project. There is no force sensor: the wrench (fx, fy, fz) is estimated from joint torques and expressed in the robot base frame, with z vertical. The recordings are not public; course staff can get them from me (see Reproduce).

Seated rates differ strongly between sessions, which matters for metric choice and for thresholds.

Key variables. About 1,600 samples per trial: time, phase (wedge or hold), the three force components with and without filtering, force magnitude, and the end-effector pose. Metadata give the rim position and wedge target of the fixture.

Quality checks (script 01): no missing values, exactly one attempt per trial, labels agree with the folder they came from, controller settings are constant. One trial has a 25.5 s wedge against a median of 4 s; it is kept and flagged rather than silently dropped. The logger records in bursts of about 55 samples at 1 kHz separated by 0.27 s gaps, which shapes the preprocessing below.

The issue that drives the project. From metadata alone, without looking at labels: between 7 Aug and 10 Aug the rim moved cm sideways and the horizontal approach direction turned from . So absolute force levels and the sideways force direction change with the session, as the chart at the top shows.

3Preprocessing, and why

  • Burst averaging. Each burst becomes one point (about 16 per hold). Samples inside a burst are not independent measurements of the slow force trend, and averaging removes the gap structure. The spread inside each burst is kept as a vibration measurement.
  • Physics-motivated features. 83 features in five groups: wedge force (W, 20), hold force (H, 47), wedge-to-hold change (X, 4), within-burst vibration (V, 7) and position (P, 5). The main set W+H+X+V has 78.
  • Exclusions. Position, rim and target coordinates and absolute pose are dropped from the main set, because they are constant within a session and would let a model recognise the session instead of the lens state.
  • Traces for distance-based models. Each phase is resampled to 15 points so that trials of different length can be compared.
  • No leakage. Median imputation, standardisation and the choice of the single best feature are fitted inside each training fold only. The distance matrices for dynamic time warping use no labels and no learned scaling, so computing them once on all trials leaks nothing.

The proposed representation: fixture-invariant hold (FIH)

I model a fixture change as acting on every force sample of a trial as f(t) ↦ Rθ f(t) + b, where Rθ rotates about the vertical axis (approach direction) and b is a constant offset (pose-dependent bias of the torque-based estimate, different preload). Both are unknown. With t0 the first hold burst:

d(t) = f(t) − f(t0),  a(t) = dz(t),  ℓ(t) = √(dx(t)2 + dy(t)2)

Proposition. a(t) and ℓ(t) do not change for any θ and b.

Proof. The offset b cancels in the difference, so d becomes Rθd. The third row of Rθ is (0, 0, 1), so a is unchanged. Its upper 2×2 block is a rotation Q, and |Qv| = |v|, so ℓ is unchanged. Burst averages and linear resampling are weighted sums with weights adding to 1, which commute with the map, so invariance survives the whole pipeline. ∎

The wedge phase is left out on purpose: it is shaped by the compliant controller, and its relation to seating reverses between sessions (section 8).

5How the models were evaluated

Both protocols were fixed before any model comparison.

  • Random cross-validation (nested). Stratified 5-fold on label × session, repeated 10 times with the same folds for every model. Inside each training fold, a 5-fold grid search picks hyperparameters by ROC-AUC, and the inner out-of-fold scores set two decision thresholds: τBA (best balanced accuracy) and τsafe (the most sensitive threshold with a false-seated rate of at most 10%). The outer fold is used only for scoring.
  • Cross-session (the deployment test). Train on one session, including the grid search and both thresholds, then test on the other session, in both directions.
  • Metrics. ROC-AUC is primary because it is threshold-free and does not depend on the very different seated rates (45% and 26%). I also report balanced accuracy at τBA, and sensitivity at τsafe with its false-seated rate, the number that matters for not cracking lenses.
  • Uncertainty. Trial-level bootstrap with 2000 resamples, paired for model differences so trial difficulty cancels, and a permutation test that shuffles labels within each session 1000 times and reruns the whole nested pipeline.

Honesty about the design process. I designed FIH after seeing the cross-session failure on these two sessions. During development three kNN trace variants were scored across sessions: . FIH was kept because the nuisance model predicts it, but its cross-session numbers are post hoc in that sense. Only a new session can give a clean test.

7Error analysis

The two kinds of unseated trial behave very differently. The table gives, under random CV at τBA, the fraction of each recorded outcome that a model calls seated: the first column is sensitivity, the other two are false-seated rates for each failure type.

Partial contact is the main safety risk. A partly seated lens also stops compressing during the hold, so its invariant response looks like a seated one. Raw kNN-DTW separates it better, but with level information that does not transfer between sessions. The explorer above shows this directly: orange dots cluster with the blue ones.

Calibration. FIH-LR ranks usefully, but its Brier score () is no better than always predicting the base rate (0.226), because class weighting shifts its probabilities up; FIH-kNN is over-confident (calibration slope ). Decisions use thresholds chosen on inner scores, so they remain valid, but the probabilities themselves would need recalibration before use.

8Deeper analysis

The derivation, checked numerically

Every raw trial gets a random rotation θ ~ U(0°, 360°) and offset b ~ N(0, 2²) N per axis, and the whole per-trial pipeline is rerun. The FIH statistics change by at most 2.7×10−15, which is floating-point rounding, while most standard features change. The script stops with an error if the change ever exceeds 10−8, so a later code edit cannot silently break invariance.

Which features can transfer at all?

A feature can only transfer if its within-session AUC lies on the same side of 0.5 in both sessions.

Wedge features mostly reverse between sessions; their mean oriented transfer AUC is below chance. SingleFeature's usual choice, the detrended spread of fy in the hold, is offset-free, and a 30° turn leaves most of it in y, which is why it is a strong cross-session baseline.

RandomForest recovers at 180° because a half turn flips the sign of fy and undoes the real flip between sessions, which independently confirms that the sideways direction is the nuisance. The FIH models do not move, as the proposition guarantees. This measures how fragile the other models are; it does not show that real fixture changes are only rotations and offsets.

Representation or learner?

Given the 14 FIH statistics, the standard learners transfer at . RandomForest gains least: with 33 to 42 training trials, the trees split on noise. Simple learners benefit most from the invariant input.

9Limitations

  • Two sessions, and the method was designed after seeing them. A stronger claim needs new sessions with new fixture placements, held out and evaluated once.
  • FIH-LR's within-session signal is weak. It does not clear the permutation null on its own (p = 0.09), so its cross-session lead rests on two test sessions and needs a new session to confirm.
  • Small test sets. Each direction tests on 33 or 42 trials; single-direction intervals are about ±0.15.
  • The nuisance model is narrow. It covers constant offsets and rotation about the vertical axis, not gain changes, tilts or offsets that drift during the hold.
  • Partial contact is often taken for success. A deployed verifier would need a depth-sensitive invariant cue, such as axial stiffness during a small probing motion.
  • Labels and setting. One operator labelled all trials, and all evaluation is offline.

Answer to the question. The force signal does discriminate seated from unseated lenses within a session, but that accuracy largely does not survive a moved fixture. Describing the hold by quantities a moved fixture cannot change restores useful cross-session ranking with the same learners, at a measured cost in within-session accuracy. With two sessions this is evidence, not proof, of transfer.
